# SCB family routing — "Where should I send my conservation paper?"

The OFFICIAL Society for Conservation Biology decision chart for choosing among the three SCB
journals. Apply this BEFORE format work whenever the target is an SCB journal, or when choosing
among them.

- **Source (OFFICIAL):** figure "WHERE SHOULD I SEND MY CONSERVATION PAPER?" on the Conservation
  Letters *For Authors* page —
  https://conbio.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/1755263x/homepage/forauthors.html
- **confidence:** official · **last_verified:** 2026-06-22
- **Applies to:** Conservation Biology (CB) · Conservation Letters (CL) · Conservation Science &
  Practice (CSP).

## Decision flow (top to bottom)

**GATE 1 — Clear, conservation-relevant theme or research question?** (includes all relevant
disciplines and interdisciplinary research)
- NO → **Don't send to an SCB journal.**
- YES → Gate 2.

**GATE 2 — Strong empirical data or systematic review, rigorous methods, clear research questions?**
- NO → **Don't send to an SCB journal.**
- YES → Relevance test.

**RELEVANCE TEST (the fork) — Does the paper have EITHER:**
- (a) **General, big-picture relevance\*** for conservation & conservation science, **OR**
- (b) **Applied research with clear conservation outcomes or methodological advances, relevant
  BEYOND local context**?
- YES → Broad-relevance branch (CL or CB).
- NO → Gate 3 (CSP or reject).

### Broad-relevance branch → Conservation Letters or Conservation Biology
- **Strong policy relevance and/or notable theory or methods advance?**
  - YES → **Best communicated as a concise article within Conservation Letters' current card limit?**
    - YES → **CONSERVATION LETTERS**
    - NO → **CONSERVATION BIOLOGY**
- **Best communicated as an in-depth article within Conservation Biology's current card limit (longer methods, more figures, complex
  arguments)?**
  - YES → **CONSERVATION BIOLOGY**

### Gate 3 (relevance test = NO) → applied / local branch
**GATE 3 — Clear, direct and explicit application to conservation practice, policy or planning for a
PARTICULAR species, location or social context?**
(Novelty and big-picture relevance are **not** requirements here; **usefulness to
practitioners/policy makers IS** a requirement.)
- YES → **CONSERVATION SCIENCE & PRACTICE**
- NO → **Don't send to an SCB journal.**

## \*What is "big-picture relevance"? (some or all apply)
- Deals with important themes that span disciplines.
- Strong basis in theory; advances or tests general understanding.
- Useful to authors around the world (Asia, Africa, Americas, Europe) and/or those working on
  different study species or systems.
- Might be interesting to an international news outlet.
- Direct relevance for important conservation problems beyond local context.
- Methods-focused paper provides a significant and transferable advance in methods or their
  applications.

> Many papers could have broader relevance if the work were better framed and grounded in theory.
> **It is the author's job to make relevance clear.**

## "Don't send to an SCB journal" — reasons SCB rejects papers
- No conservation-relevant theme or question.
- Primarily descriptive.
- Lacks a clear research question; reads like a report or summary.
- Weak methods, data, or analysis.

## One-line cheatsheet
- Rigorous + **big-picture/general** + concise + strong policy/theory/methods → **Conservation Letters**; cap comes from its current card.
- Rigorous + **big-picture/general** + in-depth → **Conservation Biology**; cap comes from its current card.
- Rigorous but **relevance is LOCAL/specific** (a particular species/place/social context), useful to practitioners/policy makers → **Conservation Science & Practice**
- Fails Gate 1/2, or purely descriptive/weak → not an SCB journal (consider e.g. Biological Conservation, or a regional/taxon-specific venue)
