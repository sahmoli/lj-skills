# Shared Inference & Epistemic Exclusions

Never assert any of the following debunked defaults as established fact across research questions, designs, reviews, or concept notes. Domain-specific ecological cautions live in `lj-evidence/references/domain-ecology.md`.

## Scientific Inference & Methodology
- **Correlation or spatial pattern establishes the generating mechanism.** → Pattern ≠ process; unobserved confounders, reverse causality, and sampling artefacts remain live until a discriminating contrast rules them out.
- **Nonlinear fine-grain relationships survive spatial or group averaging unchanged.** → Averaging and nonlinear transformations generally do not commute (Jensen's inequality, ecological fallacy, Simpson's paradox).
- **Statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) equals practical importance, or $p > 0.05$ proves zero effect.** → Report effect sizes, uncertainty intervals, denominators (never cherry-pick significant subsets), and statistical power; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence.
- **Rising proxy metrics prove target improvement.** → Goodhart's law and construct validity (Cronbach & Meehl 1955): optimized proxies decouple from underlying constructs.
- **Authoritative venue/source type guarantees claim support.** → Verify source identity, passage-level support (including denominator, unit, and direction), and scope separately.
- **Counterintuitive framing outranks accuracy.** → Accuracy, primary evidence, and discriminating power strictly precede novelty.

## Framing & Pedagogy
- **Renaming or splitting synonyms constitutes a mechanism.** → Naming ≠ explaining (Feynman 1966).
- **Every disagreement can be settled by a single experiment.** → Separate empirical predictions from normative/value premises.
- **Learning-style matching (VARK) or fixed "learning pyramid" retention percentages (10%/90%).** → Unsupported / fabricated (Pashler et al. 2008).
- **Fluent explanations or UI clicks prove learner mastery.** → Guard against the illusion of explanatory depth (Rozenblit & Keil 2002); require active prediction or transfer.
